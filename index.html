Did it come back on and off?

A reversal (symptoms returning when the exposure wore off or stopped, and going again when it came back) is the strongest evidence a single case can give.

Evidence strength by trigger

Tiers come from a six-point attribution score (documented chronic baseline, a before-and-after measure, plausible timing, confounders addressed, and two points for a reversal) plus reporting quality. A is strong; C is logged but weak, or flagged.

    Adding cases

    The data lives in data/cases.json in the Serendipity_studies repository. Claude adds cases there: it checks the identifiers, fills every field, runs the validator and pushes, and this page updates within a minute or two. The screening ledger behind it is in ledger/.

    Cases not yet fully coded are waiting on a full text that couldn't be reached from a Claude session. The full-text queue lists them by route (CKN, publisher download, library), easy wins first.
